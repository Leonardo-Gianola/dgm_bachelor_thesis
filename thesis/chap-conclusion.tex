\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}

\section{Summary}

For the conclusion of this work we can answer the research question
positively: multi-fidelity schedulers can reduce the cost per
improvement and achieve the same or better results, with some caveats.
The Genetic Algorithm is the cheapest scheduler to reach a strong
result, at \$0.71 per accuracy point, \$26 total cost over the run,
and a peak of 70.59\%. Hyperband produced the best result with the
highest peak of 75.86\%, but at 73\% higher cost than the comparable
Genetic Algorithm run. ASHA showed a visible improvement on wall time
with a reduction of about 32\% over Hyperband, but with a regressing
trajectory that ends below its own generation~0. The baseline, on the
other hand, is the only scheduler with a 100\% compile rate, at the
lowest peak of 59.57\%. All these results are single seed (n=1) and
descriptive only.

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The Genetic Algorithm's generation~1 was silently skipped by a
    \texttt{seed=0} selection bug, leaving only two productive
    generations.
  \item The thesis budget allowed only a single seed per scheduler, so
    no significance test is possible and all results are descriptive.
  \item Per-generation cost is not tracked, so cost-to-threshold
    figures use the whole run's cost and overstate the marginal cost
    of crossing each threshold.
  \item Evaluation is confined to the 50-task
    \texttt{swe\_verified\_mini} subset; results may not transfer to
    the full SWE-bench Verified set.
  \item All experiments use a single LLM
    (\texttt{openrouter/minimax/minimax-m2.5}); other models may
    interact differently with multi-fidelity schedules.
  \item ASHA's regressing trajectory may itself be a single-seed fluke
    and could shift under replication.
  \item This work introduces a coverage-reliability metric alongside
    the original resolved/submitted rate; the metric is new and not
    externally validated, even though it gives a more faithful view of
    each scheduler's actual exposure.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Work}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Multi-seed replication.} The most important next step
    is to rerun the four schedulers under several seeds for
    significance testing and a clearer comparison across schedulers.
  \item \textbf{Larger benchmarks.} Evaluating the same schedulers on
    bigger benchmarks would test how the present results change with
    more tasks and a larger search space.
  \item \textbf{Stronger language model.} This thesis fixed the LLM to
    \texttt{minimax-m2.5}; swapping in a more capable model is one of
    the most promising directions, as the interaction between
    scheduler and model may shift the ranking observed here.
\end{itemize}
